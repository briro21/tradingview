So we arrive at the question we left open at the start. If the world we see is a simulation, if the self is a story, and if civilization is skiing toward an abyss, then what is all of this for? And what is happiness?

Bach's answer begins with suffering. Suffering happens when your mind cares about things it cannot change. It keeps pushing against a wall, and the wall does not move.

And happiness? The outside world cannot make you happy, because the world itself is neutral. Rain is not sad. Money is not joyful. Only your mind decides what things mean. That is why Bach offers this line, one of the most memorable of the whole conversation: happiness is a cookie that your brain bakes for itself.

Think of the cookie a parent promises a child to get them to eat their vegetables. Your nervous system does the same thing. Pleasure and satisfaction are signals from your own operating system, rewards that keep your primate body fighting, learning and building. The cookie was never out there. It was always baked in here.

This also explains why so many people reach every goal they set and still feel empty. The promotion, the house, the bigger number in the bank account. Each one delivers a cookie for a moment, and then the nervous system raises the bar. The reward was never in the object. It was a signal, designed to keep you moving.

So stop chasing the cookie in the outside world. Instead, try to understand your own software. Notice the stories your mind tells you, the rewards it dangles, the fears it runs. Because once you see the simulation for what it is, you can stop being a character in it, and start becoming its author.
