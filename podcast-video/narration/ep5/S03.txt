For Bach, AI is much more than automated statistics. He sees it as humanity's last great philosophical project. Intelligence, at its core, is the ability to make models of reality. And the real goal of AI is a system that can model itself, a mind that finally understands what a mind is.

Seen this way, the Turing test is not really a test of how clever machines are. It is a recursive test of us. Are human beings intelligent enough to understand their own minds well enough to build one? If we can rebuild ourselves, we have finally understood ourselves.

But instead of building a wise mind, we have spent the last decade building a sick one. Bach compares social media to a global brain, billions of people wired together into one giant network. And what does that brain do? It has no stable sense of what is real, no brakes, no one in charge. He describes Twitter as a global brain in a kind of permanent seizure, constantly firing and never settling.

Each of us becomes a little reinforcement learning agent, chasing the reward of likes, outrage and empty arguments. A tiny hit of dopamine, again and again. The network learns what keeps us scrolling, not what keeps us sane.

Meanwhile, in the physical world, we use clever accounting to drain the planet, and we call it innovation. A wise global mind would look at the slope we are skiing down and change course. Our current global mind is too busy refreshing its feed.

To understand why we behave this way, Bach goes much deeper, all the way down to the smallest unit of life.
