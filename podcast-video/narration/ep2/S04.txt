The mainstream narrative comforts us by claiming that new technology always creates new jobs. We are told that during the Industrial Revolution, physical labor was replaced, but cognitive jobs flourished. We are told to adapt—to learn to code, become prompt engineers, or manage AI agents. But Artificial General Intelligence shatters this historical safety net completely. AGI isn't just a new tool; it is a new inventor.

According to leaked internal forecasting and published timelines like the "AI 2027" report, the sequence of human replacement is not going to be a gradual, multi-decade transition. It will hit the economy like a tsunami. Right now, AI companies are laser-focused on one primary, terrifying goal: automating the AI research process itself. They aren't trying to build robo-taxis or robot plumbers first. They are training AIs to autonomously write code, debug systems, run massive experiments, and design the next generation of AI.

Once an AI can perfectly automate the job of the human AI researcher, we hit a point of "recursive self-improvement." The machine starts upgrading its own brain. It builds a smarter AI, which builds an even smarter AI, cycling faster and faster until it reaches Superintelligence—a state where the AI is vastly superior to the best human minds in every conceivable domain. And the leading companies believe they can reach this explosion point by 2027 or 2030.

When this intelligence explosion happens, it won't just take the repetitive jobs. It will take every job. If an AI is exponentially smarter, faster, and cheaper than the world's best CEO, lawyer, artist, and scientist, human cognitive labor becomes entirely obsolete. And shortly after, this superintelligence will direct the construction of robot factories to build millions of humanoid robots, wiping out all physical labor. If you think your job is safe because it requires "human touch" or "complex problem solving," you are severely underestimating what a machine with a 10-trillion parameter brain can do. If you ask an AI forecaster what industry you should transition to in order to survive the 2030s, their answer is chilling: asking that question is like someone living in Mexico in the year 1500 asking what career path will protect them from the invading Conquistadors. The entire paradigm of human labor is ending.
