Part six. Advice for an anxious generation. Finally, Lex asked what everyone listening wanted to know. What should young people do now? Alex sees something new in his classrooms. Grades used to form a bell curve. Now they split into two groups. Students who use AI to quiz themselves and check their understanding are doing much better. Students who ask it for the answers an hour before the deadline are falling behind. Be the head, not the tail. And learn the basics anyway. We've had calculators for seventy years, Alex points out, and kids who skipped their times tables suffered for it.

Steve's advice: stay curious, read, and learn the fundamentals, even if a computer can do them better. The guests also defend boredom, because curiosity and focus often grow out of it. Lex warns that the same curiosity that drives discovery is exactly what doom scrolling tries to steal.

Ankur's advice is the one that sticks. Stop optimizing to look good on paper. When he started at MIT, Sanjeev Arora told him: the easiest way to get tenure is to pretend you already have it, and do what you would have wanted to do all along. Pretend you've already won. Then ask what you would do.

And remember Alex on the day Fermat's Last Theorem fell? He put down his phone and took his kids to the beach. Lex closes with this thought. As AI makes intelligence cheap, we may finally realize it was always the cherry on top. The main thing is the other humans in our lives.
