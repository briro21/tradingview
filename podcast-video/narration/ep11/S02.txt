Part two. The last money out the door.

Look at who wrote the biggest checks. Nvidia. Microsoft. Google. AMD. TSMC. Broadcom. Micron. Together, about twenty-six billion dollars into the IPO.

Notice something? These are Anthropic's suppliers. In Zitron's view, they were topping up a customer, so that customer can keep paying them, and so early investors could cash out. That's why he calls it the last money out the door. And retail investors who bought near the top, he notes, faced almost immediate losses.

Here's why that matters for everyone else. The next big AI company in line is OpenAI. It just raised eleven billion dollars, at a one point two five trillion dollar valuation. Reports say it wanted thirty billion, at one point four trillion. Zitron even found it odd that one Bloomberg report stressed the money had actually been wired and received. In years of reading funding news, he says, he'd never seen that.

And OpenAI's numbers, as he cites them, are rough. A loss of ten point nine billion dollars in one quarter, on about ten billion dollars of revenue. Yes, its margins are improving: from around minus one hundred fifty percent, to around minus one hundred. And its newest model got a cold reception, even from its usual cheerleaders.

He expects OpenAI to need more money early next year. But its biggest backers, he says, chipped in only a few hundred million this time, because they're short on cash themselves.

So his question is blunt. After Anthropic soaked up this much money, who's left to fund OpenAI?

And the biggest supplier of all just went quiet on something important.
