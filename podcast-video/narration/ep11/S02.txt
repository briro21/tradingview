Part two. The last money out the door.

Look at who wrote the biggest checks. Nvidia. Microsoft. Google. AMD. TSMC. Broadcom. Micron. Together, about $26 billion into the IPO.

Notice something? These are Anthropic's suppliers. In Zitron's view, they were topping up a customer, so it can keep paying them, and so early investors could cash out. That's the last money out the door. Retail buyers near the top, he notes, lost money almost immediately.

Here's why that matters for everyone else. Next in line is OpenAI. It just raised $11 billion, at a $1.25 trillion valuation. Reports say it wanted $30 billion, at $1.4 trillion. Oddly, one Bloomberg report stressed the money had actually been wired and received. Zitron had never seen that.

And OpenAI's numbers, as he cites them, are rough. A loss of $10.9 billion in one quarter, on about $10 billion of revenue. Yes, margins improved: from about minus 150% to minus 100%. And its newest model got a cold reception, even from its cheerleaders.

He expects OpenAI to need more money early next year. But its biggest backers, he says, chipped in only a few hundred million, because they're short on cash too.

So his question is blunt. After Anthropic soaked up this much money, who's left to fund OpenAI?

And the biggest supplier of all just went quiet on something important.
