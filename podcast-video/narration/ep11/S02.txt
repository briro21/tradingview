Part two. The last money out the door.

Here is the detail Zitron keeps coming back to. Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, AMD, TSMC, Broadcom and Micron, together, put about twenty-six billion dollars into Anthropic's IPO.

These are Anthropic's suppliers. In his view, they were pumping up a customer with cash, so that they keep getting paid, and so the venture investors could cash out. He calls it the last money out the door.

That matters, he says, because of who comes next. OpenAI just raised eleven billion dollars at a one point two five trillion dollar valuation, after reportedly hoping for thirty billion at one point four trillion. Zitron also finds it odd that one Bloomberg report stressed the money had actually been wired and received. In years of reading funding announcements, he says, he had never seen a reporter feel the need to say that.

And the numbers Zitron cites are rough. He says OpenAI lost ten point nine billion dollars in its third quarter, on about ten billion dollars of revenue. Its margins, he notes dryly, are improving: from around minus one hundred fifty percent to around minus one hundred percent.

Its newest model, he adds, got a cold reception, even from people who usually cheer every release.

So what is the pitch for OpenAI's next round, which he expects it will need in the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven? Investors would be buying a company with worse economics than Anthropic, at a similar valuation. And its biggest backers, he says, put in only a few hundred million dollars this time, because they are running low on cash themselves.

His question is simple. After Anthropic soaked up so much institutional and retail money, who actually has the money left, even if they wanted to invest?

And the biggest supplier of all has its own problem.
