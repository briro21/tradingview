Part six. Who pays?

Start with gamers. Steve doesn't expect graphics card prices to fall any time soon. And don't wait for cheap used parts, he says: these data center chips were never built for ordinary computers.

Then towns. Steve describes a data center his team visited. $77 million in tax breaks, in a relatively poor area. Fewer than 20 employees. Local hires, he says, mostly work in security, landscaping and cleaning. Once construction ends, Zitron adds, many sites need only 50 or 60 people. And the planned West Virginia site, Steve says, may clear one of America's last chestnut-producing forests. The fallout lands on the people it always lands on. Not the billionaires.

Then the economy. Zitron argues the industry's promise, that buying chips grows the economy, was oversold. Steve's version of the slogan: GPUs equal GDP. An economist told Zitron that official statistics may have overstated software's contribution to growth since 2022. That's his argument, not a settled finding.

So here's your watch list. Does Nvidia file its quarterly report by December 4th? Does Anthropic clear the $21 billion bar? Can OpenAI raise money early next year? And does a neocloud run out of road?

To be fair to the other side: Nvidia still posted record numbers, and Anthropic's shares are above their listing price. Zitron's view: those numbers rest on a confidence game, and the man who ran it is gone. This is his analysis, not financial advice.
