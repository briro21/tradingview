Part three. The missing filing.

Nvidia reported earnings this week, under interim chief executive Colette Kress, the former finance chief. On the surface: record numbers, beat expectations, raised forecast.

But Zitron spotted what wasn't there. Normally, Nvidia files its quarterly report, the ten-Q, alongside earnings. This time, nothing. The deadline, he says, is December 4th, and Kress promised it within the coming weeks.

Bulls say it's just extra checks during the investigation into Jensen Huang. Zitron isn't convinced: the scandal broke just a week before earnings, so the filing was likely nearly done. His words: we need answers from Nvidia.

On the scandal, he cites the Wall Street Journal, which he says now puts chip sales through Megaspeed at around $12 billion. Those are reports and allegations, not findings.

Then his guest, Steve from the hardware channel Gamers Nexus, pulls up Nvidia's own numbers. Revenue booked to Singapore went from about $760 million in one quarter to about $4 billion a year later. Over one fiscal year, roughly $24 billion. And then it vanished from the breakdown, after Nvidia changed how it reports revenue by region. Steve's question: a company that can tell when a driver leaks to a reviewer can't track its own chips?

One more thing doesn't add up for Zitron. In August, Nvidia promised 70% growth next year. Yet the Journal reported Jensen worried about a glut of chip-making capacity. You can't easily have both.

The board, he says, has made clear Jensen isn't coming back. So can anyone do what he did?
