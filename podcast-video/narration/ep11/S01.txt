Part one. The debut.

How did the year's biggest AI listing go? Anthropic, the maker of Claude, listed at $83 a share. As Zitron recorded, it traded higher, but struggled to stay above $100. His verdict: not the massive return people expected. But first days say little. So let's look at the business.

First, growth. Citing spending data from Ramp, he says Anthropic's growth this quarter is trending toward 15 to 25%. A quarter ago, it was 47%. A few quarters before that, around 150%. That's not a rocket anymore. That's a rocket running out of fuel.

Now the bills. Anthropic has signed up for $895 billion of commitments, mostly computing power that hasn't even started. And $435 billion of that, he says, can't be cancelled.

Here's the number that really matters. The Financial Times' Robert Armstrong calculated that for a decent return, Anthropic would need about $600 billion of revenue a year by 2030. Microsoft, with monopolies across office software, made about $331 billion last year. Anthropic would need nearly twice that.

To be fair, there's a cushion: over $200 billion in cash after the IPO, by Zitron's count. But big prepayments are coming, and he says anything under $21 billion of revenue this quarter would be a problem. Even 15% growth, in his math, isn't enough.

Which raises the obvious question. Who was buying these shares, and why?
