Part one. The debut.

So how did the biggest AI listing of the year actually go? Anthropic, the maker of Claude, listed at eighty-three dollars a share. As Zitron recorded his latest monologue, it was trading higher, but struggling to stay above one hundred. His verdict: definitely not the massive return people were expecting.

He adds a fair warning. A new stock's first days say almost nothing about the business. So let's look at the business.

First, growth. Using data from the spending platform Ramp, he says Anthropic's revenue growth this quarter is trending toward fifteen to twenty-five percent. A quarter ago, it was forty-seven. A few quarters before that, around one hundred fifty. That's not a rocket anymore. That's a rocket running out of fuel.

Now the bills. Anthropic has signed up for eight hundred ninety-five billion dollars of commitments, mostly for computing power that hasn't even started yet. And four hundred thirty-five billion of that, he says, can't be cancelled.

But here's the number that really matters. The Financial Times' Robert Armstrong calculated that to earn a decent return on all of that, Anthropic would need about six hundred billion dollars of revenue a year by twenty thirty. Microsoft, with monopolies across half of office software, made about three hundred thirty-one billion last year. Anthropic would need nearly twice that.

To be fair, it has a cushion. Zitron puts its cash at over two hundred billion dollars after the IPO. But big prepayments on those computing contracts are coming, and he says anything under twenty-one billion dollars of revenue this quarter would be a problem. Even fifteen percent growth, in his math, wouldn't be enough.

Which raises the obvious question. Who was buying these shares, and why?
