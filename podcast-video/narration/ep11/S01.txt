Part one. The debut.

Anthropic, the maker of Claude, is now a public company. Its shares listed at eighty-three dollars. As Zitron recorded his latest monologue, they were trading higher, but struggling to stay above one hundred. His verdict: definitely not the massive return people were expecting.

He is careful to add that the price of a new stock in its first days says almost nothing about the company. It's the psychology of the market. So he looks at the business instead.

Growth first. Citing data from the spending platform Ramp, Zitron says Anthropic's revenue growth this quarter is trending toward fifteen to twenty-five percent, down from forty-seven percent the quarter before. Even allowing for seasonal swings, he argues, this is no longer a company in rapid growth.

Then the bills. Anthropic has eight hundred ninety-five billion dollars of commitments, mostly for computing power that hasn't started yet. Four hundred thirty-five billion of that, he says, can't be cancelled.

He quotes a calculation from Robert Armstrong at the Financial Times: to earn a twenty percent return on all that invested capital, Anthropic would need about six hundred billion dollars of revenue a year by twenty thirty. For context, Microsoft, with monopolies across several kinds of software, made about three hundred thirty-one billion in its last fiscal year.

To be fair, Anthropic now has a huge cushion. Thanks to the IPO, Zitron puts its cash at over two hundred billion dollars. But he says big prepayments on its computing contracts are coming, and he's heard that anything less than twenty-one billion dollars of revenue this quarter would be a problem.
