Part four. Nobody can replace him.

The reassurance goes: whatever Nvidia loses, AMD and Broadcom pick up. Zitron says that misses what Jensen did.

Start with cash. Over the past year, he says, Apple generated around $136 billion of free cash flow. Nvidia, about $127 billion. Microsoft, around $76 billion. And Broadcom? About $39 billion. Roughly a quarter of Nvidia's.

Broadcom, he says, already broke what he calls Jensen's golden rule: never hold your customers' bags. He says Broadcom is backstopping around $60 billion of debt to lease chips to Anthropic. Jensen, in his view, never would have. Rivals tried copying his deals, but only Jensen had the balance sheet.

Then there were the giant headlines. The Journal now reports, he says, that the firms behind a half-trillion dollar AI fund aren't committed to raising any money, and the number isn't meaningful. Then Nvidia's $100 billion plan with OpenAI, which Jensen later called never a commitment.

And Nvidia kept writing smaller checks to keep buyers alive. Perplexity, the AI search startup, gets around $3 billion, he says, because it buys so much compute.

His conclusion: this industry ran on Jensen's word and Nvidia's balance sheet. He points to reports that Jensen told customers to collect prepayments from their own customers. To Zitron, a sign the money was running out.
