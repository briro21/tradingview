Why can't a superpower just walk away from a war it's losing? Political scientist Robert Pape says it's because war isn't chess. It's roulette. You bet on red. It comes up black. A rational player walks away. But the gambler doubles down, then borrows chips and bets even bigger. Pape maps each spin onto the Iran war: kill the Supreme Leader, a six week air campaign, a blockade. Each time, he says, Iran didn't fold. And the chips? Gas prices, lives, and political support.
