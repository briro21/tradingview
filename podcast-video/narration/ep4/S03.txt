And then, in the third week, the house gave up its first secret. In the master study, the crew was pulling up warped oak planks when the crowbar struck something that wasn't wood or stone. It was the dull, hollow thud of metal. Wedged between two load-bearing joists, buried in a century of dust, sat a heavy copper lockbox, hidden there on purpose.

Inside, there was no gold. No jewels. Just a stack of brittle letters wrapped in oilcloth, the ink faded to sepia. They were written by the original architect, three months before the mansion's grand opening in 1912. And they were not letters of triumph. They were letters of desperation. The architect was writing to a silent partner about a catastrophic shortage of money. To finish the grand facade, corners had to be cut where no one could see them.

But beneath the letters was something even stranger. Hold that thought. We'll come back to it.
