Before a resurrection, there must be a reckoning. The first job is to clear away half a century of neglect. The vines are pulled back, revealing intricate stonework that hasn't felt the sun in fifty years. Rotten timber and collapsed plaster are hauled out of the great hall, layer by layer, until the foundation of the estate is exposed.

This is the most brutal phase, where everything looks worse before it looks better. The skeleton of the mansion stands open to the sky. Scaffolding rises around it like a steel cage, holding up a fragile giant. But as the dust settles, the archways breathe again, and the flow of the rooms makes sense once more. It is no longer a ruin. It is a canvas wiped clean.
