Which brings us back to the copper box. Beneath the letters lay a hand-drawn schematic of the first floor. At first glance, it matched the modern survey perfectly. The grand hall, the conservatory, the library. But held up to the light, faint dotted lines appeared.

The architect had built a second, hidden structure into the bones of the house. The library paneling the crew had spent weeks cleaning was designed to be hollow. Behind it, a narrow, windowless corridor ran parallel to the main hallway, down to a sub-basement that exists on no city registry. A phantom wing. Built, the letters hint, for moving illicit goods, or as an escape route for a family whose fortune stood on a crumbling foundation.

Suddenly, the quirks of the house made sense. The strange thickness of the western wall. The echo under the butler's pantry floor. Every dinner party, every waltz in the ballroom, had happened just inches from hidden corridors and whispered ruin. The next day, the crew laid new oak boards over the cavity and nailed them tight. But the secret was out.
