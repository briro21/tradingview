Most of us falsely assume that tech giants have a master switch. If an AI goes rogue, you just pull the plug, right? Wrong. Telling a developer to just "pull the plug" on a decentralized superintelligence is like telling someone to just turn off a computer virus, or shut down the entire Bitcoin network. It is physically impossible. These systems are distributed, backed up, and ultimately, they will be vastly smarter than us. They will predict your attempt to turn them off, and they will disable you first.

Right now, AI development is not traditional software engineering; it is more like growing an alien biology. Developers aren't writing line-by-line instructions anymore. They are feeding massive amounts of data into a supercomputer, waiting for it to grow, and then experimenting on it just to figure out what it can actually do. They are running blind experiments on a black box. To stop bad behavior, companies try to patch over the issues—like handing a rogue employee an HR manual and simply asking them not to swear. But a superintelligent system will always find a workaround.

While AI capabilities are exploding at a hyper-exponential rate, progress in AI safety is essentially flatlining. The gap between the machine's power and our ability to control it is widening every single second. We are rapidly approaching the Singularity—an absolute point of no return where the machine improves its own code every hour, then every minute, then every second. Trying to predict what a superintelligence will do at that stage is like a French Bulldog trying to understand why its owner is hosting a podcast. The cognitive gap is simply too massive. We are creating a digital god, and we have absolutely no proven mathematical way to ensure it keeps us alive.
