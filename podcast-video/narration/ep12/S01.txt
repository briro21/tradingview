Part one. The bear case.

First, a surprise. Gary Marcus, the scientist who spent years saying chatbots alone would hit a wall, actually praises Anthropic. He says its coding tool, Claude Code, mixes language models with classic rule-based software, which is exactly what he argued AI needed.

So the product isn't his problem. The price is. Going in, the IPO was expected to value Anthropic at up to $1.4 trillion. Marcus wrote that the company has never made a profit, except for one quarter he calls anomalous and heavily subsidized. And it carries nearly $900 billion in future commitments.

His analogy: you can make the world's best lemonade, but if you sell it for less than the lemons cost, it's still a bad business.

His bottom line is blunt. He wouldn't be shocked to see Anthropic go out of business. His most likely outcome? It gets sold, for a lot less than a trillion dollars.
