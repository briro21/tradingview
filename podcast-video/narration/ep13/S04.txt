Part four. The one number.

Damodaran's first rule of corporate finance: young companies that aren't making money shouldn't borrow. Anthropic, he says, broke it by signing hundreds of billions in compute commitments without the earnings to back them.

What buys time is cash. After the IPO, he expects about $200 billion, enough to cover the next two or three years of those bills. But only if revenue roughly doubles every year in the near term.

That's the one number he says drives everything. If revenue comes in, profits come, the commitments get paid, and the risk goes away. If it doesn't, everything else falls apart.

Host Ed Elson pointed out that growth was already slowing, from 47% a quarter toward 15 to 25%. Damodaran blames competition: OpenAI underpricing, and cheaper open models. Most businesses don't need Claude, he says. It's overkill. His advice: build models powerful enough, but not too powerful.

And even if AI helps cure diseases, he warns, the value may flow to all of us, not to Anthropic's shareholders.

So why hasn't he written it off?
