Part three. Designed in hell.

Most tech founders keep control with special voting shares. Anthropic went further. Damodaran describes a single founder share, held by seven founders, carrying 50.01% of the votes. He calls the structure one designed in hell for shareholders.

His comparison: the golden shares governments kept when they privatized companies in the 1980s and 90s, like in Margaret Thatcher's Britain. One share, but a veto over the big decisions.

And he sees a twist the founders may not have thought through. If AI ever causes a scandal, an activist government could target that one share. Take it, pay the price of an ordinary share, and suddenly control the company's biggest decisions. The founders could fight that in court, he says. But it paints a target.

There's a practical problem too. If the business needs to change course, who can make the founders listen? Maybe only a falling stock price, which he calls a blunt instrument.

So what would make the business itself work?
