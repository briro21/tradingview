We have always been comforted by the historical promise that new technology simply creates new jobs. When we invented the wheel, we needed cart drivers. During the Industrial Revolution, we needed factory workers to supervise the machines. But Artificial General Intelligence—or AGI—shatters this historical safety net entirely. We are no longer just inventing a new tool for humans to use; we are inventing a new inventor. We are building a replacement for the human mind itself.

By the year 2027, the capability to replace almost every single cognitive task will be fully realized. Think about the speed of this evolution. Just three years ago, large language models struggled to multiply three-digit numbers. Today, they are winning global mathematics olympiads and working on millennial-level scientific proofs. By 2030, humanoid robots connected to these super-brains will wipe out the need for physical labor. Plumbers, builders, chefs—all automated.

It genuinely does not matter if you are an Uber driver confidently navigating the chaotic streets of New York, a tenured university professor, or a high-level digital creator. The idea of "retraining" for a new economy is a complete myth. There is no 'Plan B' to fall back on when a silicon brain can do everything better, faster, and exponentially cheaper than you. Sure, maybe a billionaire like Warren Buffett will keep a human accountant purely as a nostalgic novelty—like buying a handmade artisanal craft instead of a mass-produced product—but for 99% of humanity, the job market will simply cease to exist. We are hurtling toward a future where our basic needs might be met through infinite digital labor and extreme wealth abundance, but it forces a deeply uncomfortable, existential question: What do you do with your life when you have 80 extra hours a week and absolutely no economic purpose in society?
