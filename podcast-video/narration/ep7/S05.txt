Part five. Troops, cyber attacks, and world war three.

In Britain, as the host points out, the government has launched a "Be Ready" campaign. Every household is told to keep three days of supplies: water, tinned food, a battery radio, and physical cash, because ATMs may not work.

Why? Cyber attacks. Pape points to a summer outage in Minnesota where power and water went down. He says a New York Times report quoted US intelligence saying it had Iran's fingerprints. And he finds it telling that the story was never followed up.

Next on his board: troops in American streets. Pape says the president has refused to rule out invoking the Insurrection Act, which would let him use the military inside the country against its own citizens. Normally a law called posse comitatus prevents exactly that, because, as Pape puts it, that's what authoritarian states do.

Back in Iran, he expects the bombing to leave a weakened but still dangerous military. That gives a reason to send in ground troops, like the Marines already moving, to hunt down the remnants. And once you go that far, Pape says, you're in a forever war. A real emergency, more emergency powers, and in his words, a devastating twenty twenty seven.

And Russia? If cyber attacks hit Europe hard, Pape expects the public to demand payback. And when Europe strikes Moscow directly, he says, that is what world war three looks like.

He's careful here. We are not in world war three today. Proxy wars, like the West funding Ukraine, happened almost every year of the Cold War. World war means the largest powers attacking each other's homelands directly. And Pape stresses this is a pathway, not a certainty. Which is why he spends the end of the conversation on how to stop it.
