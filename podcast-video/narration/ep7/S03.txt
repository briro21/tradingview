Part three. The three day plan.

According to Pape, there is a plan to hit targets in Iran over three days. Reports say the military has been told to be ready to execute within ninety six hours of a go order. And embassy families have already been pulled out of several Gulf states.

The media, he says, heard "three days" and assumed it was minor. Pape disagrees. The leaked targets, leadership, refineries, bridges, energy, look to him exactly like Instant Thunder, the plan that opened the nineteen ninety one war against Iraq. It was also meant to last three days. It went on for forty three, and never paralyzed Iraq's military.

And here's the paradox. The whole point of a plan like this is to paralyze the government. But if you succeed, who is left to negotiate with?

And if you don't fully succeed, Pape expects the remaining forces to lash back on their own, at airports and infrastructure across the region. He points out that Iran's allies in Yemen, the Houthis, have already struck Riyadh's international airport, which he calls the first intentional targeting of civilians in this war.

Then there are civilians in Iran. Pape estimates the nineteen ninety one campaign killed between five and fifteen thousand civilians. And deaths like that, he warns, lead to something harder to stop: inspired attacks.

An inspired attack isn't ordered by any government. It's an individual acting on his own, fueled by calls for revenge. Pape, who has studied eight thousand suicide attacks, points to a flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv, where the co pilot attacked the pilot with a crash axe just thirty minutes from landing. A passenger who happened to be a pilot landed the plane. Pape can't be certain, but based on the timing, he puts the odds that it was a planned suicide attack at around ninety percent.
