Part six. Three firebreaks.

Remember the wildfire? You can't stop every spark. But you can build firebreaks. And Pape names three.

Firebreak one. Every member of Congress, old and newly elected, says one thing: no escalation in Iran until the new Congress is seated, unless Iran strikes first.

Firebreak two. Every governor and mayor calls for a two month pause on federally armed agents in their cities, unless they ask for them. Take the key sparks off the table while votes are counted.

Firebreak three. The countries acting as mediators, Pakistan, Qatar and India, call on everyone to do one thing: hands off civilians. No direct targeting of civilian infrastructure or homes.

So should you stockpile water? Pape says he isn't. On day one you drink one bottle. By day three you're out. And the attacks can come again. Three days of supplies doesn't buy real time. The firebreaks do.

So what can an ordinary person do? Pape says: email, text, or call your political leaders, whoever they are, and ask them to adopt those three firebreaks. Otherwise, we're just letting events control us.

And he believes conversations like this one matter. After his last appearance on the show, he says, momentum for a major strike on Iran's drone network faded, and he credits public attention as one of the reasons.

Asked if he's a pessimist, Pape says the opposite. He lays out the dark road, not because he wants the doom loop, but because he wants us to choose a better one.
